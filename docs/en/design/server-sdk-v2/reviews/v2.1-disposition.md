## Justifications for everything that is not plain "accepted"

* **IS-C2 (partial).** Accepted: `system.env.set` is explicit-only on the allow side, never inside `system.*`/`group:system`/any preset (`legacy_compat` only with `EBX_SERVER_LEGACY_ENV_SET=1`), writes only a per-server overlay and refuses `PATH`, `LD_*`, `EBX_*`.
  Not adopted: making *every* `commands.*`/`files.*` op explicit-only. That would make presets and the "group switches" requirement unusable and would not stop the real attack (a scope-less grant); the same risk is closed more precisely by **scope-required** (D27):
  an allow of any path/command/url/agent op without its scope is refused at request time and is a lint error. Explicit-only is kept for ops whose effect is administrative or irreversible: admin-effect ops, `core.reload`, `system.env.set`, `terminal.create`, `process.signal`,
  `agents.<a>.install`, `agents.<a>.sessions.create`.
* **IS-L4 (partial).** `npm ci` and the Playwright downloader do not honour a host allowlist from outside. 0.3.0 enforces `hosts` for `tarball`, points `npm` at the configured registry only, and makes `lock` validate every `resolved` URL and integrity hash; Playwright manifests are post-0.3.0.
* **IS-S1 (deferred).** The loopback key-injecting proxy (`llm.proxy`) and per-session key minting are new components with their own threat model; they are scheduled after 0.3.0 and tracked in 10 s3.2 and risk R12. 0.3.0 states the exposure (`doctor`, `explain`) and constrains models and turns via `AgentScope`.
* **IF-10 (partial).** The review asked for *one* range and for the in-flight class names and attributes to survive; both are done. Its recommended range (E9xxx) is rejected: the working-tree range is part of the **unreleased** 0.3.0, the user-level decision is that the server plane lives in the execution family `E31xx` and that
  providers keep `E8000-E8030`, so renaming `E9000-E9005` costs nothing and removes a fourth scheme. The in-flight change list is 07 s4 and 10 s9-0.
* **IF-29 (rejected).** Collapsing the five adapters' ops into one family with an `adapter` argument would remove per-adapter policy addressability (`allow("agents.codex.run", principals=["role:dev"])`), which is a stated product requirement, and it would break the two-level gate. The catalog growth is bounded (4 ops per adapter, 105 ops total, generated) and offset by the
  `agents.<a>.**` selector sugar and by generation (04 s11, 05 s3).
* **IF-30 (partial).** The cut list (10 s3.2) takes the proposals that do not conflict with the stated requirements: no rollback dispatcher, no shipped compat package, no alias window, fewer manifest types, fewer CLI subcommands, no hot reload, later TS client, `llm.proxy` later. Not taken: shipping fewer adapters. The stated requirement is codex, claude_code, qwen_code, cursor and qoder in 0.3.0;
  the last three are flagged **experimental** instead.
* **B-14 (rejected as proposed).** The review asked to document what `ctx.paths` contains when the winning statement has no `PathScope`. The better fix removes the case: a statement without a scope for a declared path/command/url parameter does not admit (`policy.scope_required`), so `ctx.paths[name]` is always the canonical admitted value (02 s3, 03 s15.5).

## Where the spike rewiring matters (PoC integrity)

Seven PoCs originally tested **inline naive code** (a local f-string, a local glob helper, a hand-rolled URL check), not the design. They were rewired to the spike's real components so a HELD result means the design holds, not that the PoC tested itself:
IS-P8 (message built from a missing key), IS-F1 (uses `presets.readonly()` and `PathScope.anywhere` against `/proc`), IS-F2 (catches the guard's `Denied`), IS-C2b (the escape is now rejected at `CommandScope` construction), IS-C6 (runs through `api.start_http`, which calls `harden_process()`),
IS-A8 (raw socket targets against the real listener, expecting 400), IS-U1/IS-U1b (real `UrlScope` and `block_private`). Their final status is HELD; they are the regression tests WP-3/WP-1 port (10 WP-3, WP-1).
